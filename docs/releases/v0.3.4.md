Fixes found by running the design loop on a real project model (2,100 beams). They change results: read the first two sections before comparing with an earlier run.

## Fixed: where a beam line is supported

The deflection check decided whether the end of a span was supported by counting the members at the joint, and joined the pieces of a line through every joint without a column. On the real model this gave three kinds of wrong result:

- a beam line that ends on a girder ETABS has not split there was read as a cantilever (39 lines);
- an edge beam resting on several girders was joined into one span of 45 m and more, with a limit to match;
- a cantilever girder was called supported because an edge beam sat on its tip.

In the design loop the first made beams grow at every iteration without ever passing, up to 900 x 2000.

The whole line is now one chain, cut where it is supported: at a column below or a wall, where the shear of the full service load jumps up (a girder carrying the line), and at an end that the diagrams show pushed up. The results say which members were checked together, as a span or as a cantilever, and over what length.

## New: the load path, from the shear and moment diagrams

Whether a beam carries or is carried is now read from the diagrams of the analysis, not from the framing alone. Under gravity load, a beam end that is pushed up is supported, whatever the framing looks like: an edge beam carries the girders that end on it when their ends are pushed up, even though it reaches no column itself. A downward force on an end does not settle it: a free tip carrying a beam and an end held down by a girder or a column in tension have the same diagrams. There the deflected shape of the analysis decides, read from the joint displacements: a free tip drops, a held end stays. Where there is nothing to read, the framing decides, by how directly each line reaches a column or wall.

It is used in three places:

- **Support status.** A girder whose end drops under a downward force is a cantilever, whatever beam is at its tip. Before, any beam there made it "supported both ends".
- **Deflection.** As above.
- **Carrier depth.** The carrier of a beam end is the beam that pushes it up.

A planted column is a load on the beam under it, not a support.

## Design loop

- **A deflection that growing does not help.** A beam whose deflection ratio comes down by less than 5 % after it was made larger keeps its size and is reported with both ratios. It is the rotation or the movement of its support that governs, and no size fixes that.
- **One size along a beam line**, with two more conditions: the spans compared are those between supports, not ETABS member lengths, and a member takes the size of the one that governs only when its own moment and shear are at least 75 % of it (a loop setting; 0 switches it off). The pieces of one span always take one size.
- **Width against depth.** A new loop setting, the smallest beam width as a share of its depth, 40 % by default (30 % is the least for a special moment frame). A beam made deeper is made wider in the same step when the depth needs it.

## Model check, analysis and drift

- **A base below the ground level is accepted.** `check` wanted the seismic and wind patterns to start at the ground level and failed a model whose base is the bottom of the foundation. That is now reported for you to confirm, and the weight W and the height hn are checked over the stories of the patterns themselves. Patterns that start above the ground level still fail.
- **A top story with no structure fails.** With an empty story above the roof as the top of the seismic patterns, hn and the period cap are too long and the base shear too small (15 % on the real model).
- **The period message names its height:** "T_A 0.905 s for hn 28.60 m (LG - FT to PD2)". `analyze` uses the same height.
- **The zone 4 minimum holds for drift.** `check` failed the drift patterns that ETABS had rightly kept at 0.8 Z Nv I / R (Eq. 208-11). NSCP 208.6.5.2 lifts only the 0.11 Ca I minimum and the period cap.
- **`drift` lists every story**, not only the worst one of each load case.

## What to expect on an existing model

- Some beams that failed deflection now pass, and some that passed now fail: long joined spans had a loose limit, and cantilevers with an edge beam were not checked as cantilevers.
- Some girders change from "Supported Both Ends" to "Cantilever", which changes their seismic design.
- Long cantilever girders may fail deflection mostly from the rotation of their support. The loop reports them and does not enlarge them without end.

## Download and run

1. Download `sdt-0.3.4-windows.zip` below and unzip it. Keep the files of the `sdt` folder together.
2. Double-click `sdt.exe`, then run `doctor` first.

No Python is needed. ETABS is needed for the ETABS commands, and a LaTeX distribution such as MiKTeX for the PDF reports.
