The beam deflection check can take the slab beside a beam as its flange: T- and L-beams, NSCP 406.3.2.1. Asked for while redesigning the beams of the RP Antipolo Exp 2 top building.

## Deflection of T- and L-beams

- **A new question.** `sdt beams`, `sdt deflection` and `sdt design` ask the section of the deflection check: **Rectangular**, the beam alone (the default, as before), or **T- and L-beams**. The answer is remembered.
- **The slab beside each beam is read from the model:** which sides have a slab, its thickness, and the clear distance to the next parallel beam. Openings are left out, and beams at any angle are read.
- **The flange width is that of NSCP Table 406.3.2.1.** With slab on both sides, each overhang is the least of 8h, sw/2 and ln/8. With slab on one side, it is the least of 6h, sw/2 and ln/12.
- **The flanged section gives the stiffness.** Ig, Mcr = fr Ig / yt (yt to the tension face, NSCP 424.2.3.5b) and, under a sagging moment, the cracked section with the flange in compression all come from it. Under a hogging moment the cracked section is the web; the slab bars are left out, which is the safe side.
- **Only the deflection changes.** The strength design, the bars and the schedules do not.
- **Results:** the results and the calculation report name each beam's section, for example `T-beam: bf 2,900 mm, hf 150 mm`. A beam with no slab beside it, such as a tie beam, stays rectangular.

On the RP Antipolo top building (1,961 beams), the beams failing deflection went from 158 to 97. The governing ratios dropped by a median of 39 %.

## Download and run

1. Download `sdt-0.15.0-windows.zip` below and unzip it. Keep the files of the `sdt` folder together.
2. Double-click `sdt.exe`, then run `doctor` first.

No Python is needed. ETABS is needed for the ETABS commands, and a LaTeX distribution such as MiKTeX for the PDF reports.
