`sdt model` covers the whole slab on floors with large openings and odd-shaped bays. Found while building the RP Antipolo Exp 2 top building, where up to 1,000 m² of a floor was left without slab.

## `sdt model`: the framing

- **Light wells are voids.** An opening of 30 m² or more is a void even inside one bay: its edge gets edge beams, as for an opening that crosses girders (before, it got four trimmer beams only).
- **Voids are tied to the frame.** Beams run from each corner of a void, and about every 6 m along its edge, out along the axes of the bay to the first member within the long cantilever length. Without them, the slab around a void could be left as one ring-shaped bay that was not framed.
- **Bays half on the slab are framed.** A bay at least half on the slab counts, even when its middle point falls off it (an L-shaped or curved edge bay).
- **Bays that are mostly opening are not slab.** Before, a bay was dropped only when its middle point fell inside an opening.

## `sdt model`: the slab in ETABS

- **Slab pieces stop at the openings.** The edges of the openings cut the slab into pieces as the members do, and each piece goes to the bay it shares the most area with. Before, a piece whose middle point fell outside its bay was left out.
- **No slits or spikes.** A piece whose outline ran out and straight back on itself (where an opening touches it) was refused by ETABS and left out. Such edges are now taken off.

## Download and run

1. Download `sdt-0.14.1-windows.zip` below and unzip it. Keep the files of the `sdt` folder together.
2. Double-click `sdt.exe`, then run `doctor` first.

No Python is needed. ETABS is needed for the ETABS commands, and a LaTeX distribution such as MiKTeX for the PDF reports.
