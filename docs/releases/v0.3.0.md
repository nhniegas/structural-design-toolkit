## New: target ratios

The code passes a check when demand / capacity is at most 1.00. You can now set a lower target, as a margin of your own, for each check of each member type:

| Member type | Checks |
|---|---|
| Girders | Flexure; shear and torsion; deflection |
| Beams | Flexure; shear and torsion; deflection |
| Columns | Axial load and bending (P-M); shear; joint shear; strong column - weak beam (a minimum, at least 1.2) |

- `beams`, `columns` and `design` ask: use the code limits, use the targets saved for this model, or set them.
- The members are designed to the target: with 0.85 on flexure the bars keep Mu / φMn at or below 0.85. A check fails above its target, and the failure text says so.
- In `design`, a member above its target is made larger, and "make smaller" is measured against the target.
- The targets are saved with the model and listed in the summary and the calculation reports.

A flexure target below 1 on girders adds beam bars, which raises the column capacity shear Ve and the joint shear demand. The summary says so when such a target is set.

## New: depth of a beam that carries others

`beams` and `design` ask whether a girder or beam must be at least as deep as the beams that frame into it. When yes, a shallower carrier fails with both depths shown, and `design` makes it deeper and never makes a carrier shallower than the beams it carries. It is a detailing rule of your own, off unless you ask for it.

## Also

- A failing joint shear check now says why (above its strength, above the target, or the joint dimension rule), where it only said "FAIL".

## Download and run

1. Download `sdt-0.3.0-windows.zip` below and unzip it. Keep the files of the `sdt` folder together.
2. Double-click `sdt.exe`, then run `doctor` first.

No Python is needed. ETABS is needed for the ETABS commands, and a LaTeX distribution such as MiKTeX for the PDF reports.
