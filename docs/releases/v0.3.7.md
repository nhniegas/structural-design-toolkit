Beam sizing in the design loop: a guard for deflections that no size can fix, and a choice for the sizes along a beam line.

## New: a beam that no size can bring under its deflection limit is stopped at once

The guard of v0.3.4 asked whether the last enlargement helped by at least 5 %. A long cantilever whose deflection comes mostly from the rotation of its support still gains 6 to 8 % a step, so it passed the guard every time and grew to the largest size, still failing. On a real model 82 beams ended 2 m deep that way, with the members of their lines and the girders that carry them.

The loop now asks, before it makes a beam larger for deflection, whether any size can pass:

- The results have a new value, **Δ / limit from the rotation of the support alone**. It is the same check on the movement a rigid rotation of the support gives the cantilever, with no bending of the span: the part no section takes away.
- The rest of the ratio is the bending of the span, which falls with the stiffness b h³.
- The best the beam can reach is the support part plus the bending part at the largest width and depth allowed. When that is still over the limit, the beam keeps its size and is reported with both numbers, under "Not fixed by a larger size (deflection: the support rotates or moves)".

Replayed on that model at its sizes before the loop: 571 beams fail deflection; 77 of them, all cantilevers, are stopped at the first iteration, and the other 494 may grow. The older 5 % guard stays for the rest.

Such a beam is a framing matter, not a size matter: look at what holds its support, or at whether a support is missing.

## New: the sizes along a beam line are your choice

`sdt design` asks whether the beams and girders of one line take one size.

- **Yes** (as before): a member that is made larger passes its size to the members of its line that are held the same way, have a similar span, run nearly straight on and carry a comparable moment and shear. The next dialog sets those three limits, as before.
- **No**: every member takes the size it needs by itself. A line can then change size from one span to the next, and the three limits are not asked.

The pieces ETABS cuts one span into take one size either way: a girder is not stepped between its supports. The choice is written in the log and in the summary.

## Download and run

1. Download `sdt-0.3.7-windows.zip` below and unzip it. Keep the files of the `sdt` folder together.
2. Double-click `sdt.exe`, then run `doctor` first.

No Python is needed. ETABS is needed for the ETABS commands, and a LaTeX distribution such as MiKTeX for the PDF reports.
